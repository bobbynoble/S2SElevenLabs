NHS Hospital Reception Interpreter — Testing Guide

Help us test the interpreter

About 10 minutes. You'll play both the receptionist and the patient on one device, try a couple of languages, and tell us what happened. No account or install needed.

1Open the app

nhsspeech.ngrok.dev

Open it on a phone, tablet, or laptop with a working microphone. When your browser asks for microphone permission, choose Allow — without it, nothing will work.

🔇 Test somewhere reasonably quiet if you can. Background noise (a busy ward, a TV) makes it harder for the app to hear you clearly, which is exactly the kind of thing we want to know about — but it helps to know that's why something didn't work.

2Start a conversation

  1. Tap New Patient.
  2. Choose This tablet (you'll play both people on the one screen — simplest for testing solo).
  3. Pick a language for the patient and a language for the receptionist (English is a safe default for the receptionist side).
  4. Tap Start conversation.
📸 Good moment for a screenshot if the language list looks wrong or a language you expected isn't there.

3Have the conversation

Press and hold the green Hold to talk button under Receptionist, say something, then let go. Wait for the reply, then do the same under Patient — speaking in the patient's language this time. Go back and forth a few times like a real check-in conversation.

Not sure what to say? A few starters:

RoleTry saying
Receptionist"Hello, thanks for coming in. What's the trouble today?"
Patient"I've had a headache since yesterday and I feel a bit dizzy."
Receptionist"Okay, take a seat and someone will see you shortly."
🔊 Turn your volume up — replies play as speech, not just text on screen.

4End and finish up

Tap End Session. You'll see an estimated usage figure and, after a short pause, suggested clinical codes based on the conversation — that's expected, not a bug. Tap Back to reception desk when you're done.
